##Business Problem

The business needs to understand customer acquisition, retention, churn, and lifetime value to identify which customers and acquisition channels contribute most to long-term revenue and profit.
The analysis aims to uncover customer behavior patterns and recommend strategies to increase repeat purchases, reduce customer churn, and improve customer value.


##Key findings

11,887 customers placed 26,326 orders worth $55.76M. Average order value is $2,118 and average customer lifetime value is $4,690.

61.2% of customers order a second time, but only 9.1% within 90 days. The median wait for the second order is 367 days.

Monthly cohort retention is flat at about 3% (month 1: 3.23%, month 12: 3.81%).

43.5% of customers are At Risk and 32.0% are Churned. Only 7.3% are Loyal, and they are worth $10,475 each, against $1,956 for New customers.

In-store customers (80% of the base) are worth slightly more than Online ($4,759 vs $4,423) and repeat more often (61.8% vs 58.8%).

The US is 48% of customers and the most valuable ($5,235 per customer). Australia and France are the lowest (about $3,470).

New customers dropped from 2,832 in 2019 to 947 in 2020.


##Recommendations:

 1. Avoid aggressive short-term (30/60-day) promotional blasts. Schedule automated, personalized re-engagement campaigns around
 Months 7 to 8 (Days 210–240) to align with the natural repurchasing cycle for electronics.

2. Deploy targeted win-back campaigns specifically for customers approaching Day 300 of inactivity to intervene before they cross into full churn (>730 days).

3. Introduce post-purchase cross-selling (e.g., warranties, accessories, upgrades) between 3 and 6 months after the initial purchase to incentivize repeat orders sooner.

4. Prioritize in-store customer onboarding (e.g., digital receipt sign-ups, loyalty program creation at POS) while using digital channels to drive higher-margin accessory sales.
